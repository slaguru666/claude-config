## 2026-11-04 — FIXED POINT: a Ringworld case for The Custodians

- **New scenario, outline + handouts shipped.** `docs/scenarios/FIXED_POINT.md` in RingBRP, pack
  `fixedpoint`. Earth's axis walks in discrete steps; the Bureau triangulates a locus; the agents
  cross and **are not told where they are** for forty minutes. The ring's station-keeping is
  correcting against a mass that is not there, because something filed itself into the machine's
  list of what exists.
- **The premise is Niven's own flaw, not an invention.** A ring is in equilibrium but not stable
  equilibrium — the objection MIT students chanted at him in 1971 — and *The Ringworld Engineers*
  answers it with attitude jets. The case asks what the correcting machine is reading.
- **The ending is filing, which is what made it a Custodians case rather than a space opera.**
  Strike the row out and the thing is unaccounted for (the epilogue is a new row in the Bureau's
  own ledger); set its mass to zero and it stays filed and located. Nothing can be fought and
  there is no stat block.
- **Nine printed handouts, three of them computed charts**, from `tools/fixedpoint-handouts.mjs`
  — obliquity staircase, impulse trace, bearing plot. The data lives in the tool so the numbers
  and the pixels cannot drift; `--check` asserts the deadline still exists.
- **Twenty pen-and-ink plates, 1970s paperback interior.** The night arch is the best plate in
  the repo. **The identical composition in daylight came back with an empty sky** — a controlled
  result, so the variable is contrast, not phrasing: *a description of faintness renders as
  absence.* Plates 1 and 8 re-prompted accordingly.
- Two guard lessons worth keeping: `check-figures` reports this document at **0** figures, which
  means it cannot see degrees or kilometres and the arithmetic has no guard — checked by hand,
  and the first draft had the ring's radius printed as its diameter. And `&ldquo;`/`&rdquo;` in
  handout HTML fuse onto the adjacent word and make `check-handouts` report false drift.
